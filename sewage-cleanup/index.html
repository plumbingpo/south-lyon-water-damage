Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. When it happens in South Lyon, South Lyon Restoration is available 24/7 with technicians trained for this work, proper protective gear and disinfectants registered with the EPA, everything needed for a safe decontamination.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • Running a sink or washer and hearing a toilet or nearby drain gurgle
  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • Multiple drains in your home backing up at the same time

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Handling raw sewage safely takes specialized training, EPA-registered disinfectants that consumers cannot buy, and personal protective equipment, meaning respirators, Tyvek suits and protective boots and gloves. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. Simply placing sewage-contaminated drywall, carpet and insulation in your regular trash is both illegal and hazardous to sanitation workers and the community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

Common Sewage Cleanup Questions

Keep people and pets out of the affected area. Any water sent down a drain adds to the problem, so hold off on toilets, taps and draining appliances. Turn off power and the HVAC system to the affected area only if you can do so without touching contaminated water. Skip the mop and bucket, scrub any exposed skin and wait for a trained crew.
Typical pricing for this work in South Lyon is $2,000 to $10,000 and up, with the size of the affected area and the degree of contamination setting the number. Small contained backups may cost $1,500 to $3,000, while extensive sewage flooding can exceed $15,000 including material replacement and reconstruction.
Standard homeowner's insurance often does NOT cover sewage backup unless you have a specific sewage or water backup endorsement. The coverage usually runs only $50 to $100 per year and gives critical protection, so our strong recommendation is to add it. Look at your policy documents, or call your agent to confirm.
Most backups start with a blockage in the home's sewer line. Grease buildup, wipes and other items that should not be flushed, and tree roots growing into the pipe are the usual causes. Other possibilities are a failed old pipe and a municipal sewer overloaded by heavy rain, which sends sewage up through floor drains. To know for certain, have the line camera-inspected once cleanup is finished.
Removing the sewage and decontaminating the property is usually done in 1 to 3 days. Complete restoration, including material replacement, may take 1 to 3 weeks depending on the extent of damage. We prioritize getting your property safe for occupancy as quickly as possible.

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home
  • Clear tree roots near sewer lines, which can infiltrate and block the pipes
  • Hair and other debris are easy to stop with simple strainers on tub, shower and sink drains
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs
