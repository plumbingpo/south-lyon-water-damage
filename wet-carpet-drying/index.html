Wet Carpet Drying: What South Lyon Homeowners Should Know

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. Up top the fibers may seem barely damp, yet the pad has filled up the way a sponge does and the subfloor under it has gotten wet as well. A shop vacuum and a stack of towels only deal with the surface. Our team in South Lyon extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. Sewage or outside flood water means carpet and pad both come out.

Warning Signs

  • Days after the water was cleaned up, the carpet is cool and clammy to the touch
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or leaves your socks damp when you walk on it
  • As the carpet dries, yellowish or brown stains wick to the top of the pile
  • Ripples or loose spots where the carpet has let go of the tack strip
  • Under the wet section, the floor gives a little or squeaks when you step on it
  • A carpet corner where the backing comes apart from the face when pulled up

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Map the Wet Area

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Extract the Water

We use weighted extraction tools that squeeze water from carpet and pad at once. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry All the Layers

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

24 to 48 hours

Odor and Mold Begin

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

Ripples appear and the carpet delaminates because the latex in its backing is breaking down. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

Past one week

Past Saving

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Subfloor Damage

Left wet, the OSB or particleboard beneath a carpet swells and then crumbles. Weeks later, with the spill long forgotten, you get raised seams, squeaks and soft spots.

Stains From Furniture

When carpet is wet, the finish on wooden legs and the rust from metal glides transfer into it. Those marks are often permanent, and the furniture itself wicks water and swells.

Soaked Pad Underneath

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. It keeps feeding moisture into the carpet above and the floor below long after the surface feels dry.

Mold Under the Carpet

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. The odor arrives a long time ahead of any visible sign on top.
