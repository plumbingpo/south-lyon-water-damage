Hardwood Floor Water Damage: What South Lyon Homeowners Should Know

Wood floors react to water quickly. Moisture enters each board through its underside and edges, the boards swell and press together, and cupping begins as the edges lift above the middle. Cupping alone is no reason to give up on the floor. Solid hardwood that is dried in the first few days often flattens back out. What matters is removing moisture from the wood and from its subfloor steadily and under control.

Drying mats are how our team handles wet wood floors. The mats are sealed to the surface and connected to a vacuum unit that draws moisture up through the boards and seams, while dehumidifiers keep the room air dry. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. No sanding should happen before the wood has dried completely. A cupped floor sanded early ends up crowned afterward.

Signs to Look For

  • Baseboards or door trim swelling where the floor meets the wall
  • Sections of flooring that have risen, forming a tent shape along a seam
  • Flooring that gives under your weight in the area that got wet
  • Earthy odor at floor level even though the surface looks clean
  • Board centers humped higher than the edges, called crowning
  • Gaps opening between boards after a floor has dried too fast
If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Goes Under

Water slips through the seams and spreads between the boards and subfloor. You can towel off the top and still have a saturated underside.

24 to 48 hours

Edges Begin to Rise

Swelling sets in and board edges lift. A cloudy look can appear in the finish. The bare underside of the boards and the subfloor are where mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Boards Start Lifting

Swelling builds pressure across the floor. Edges get crushed together, nails work loose and parts of the floor may start rising from the subfloor.

After a week

Beyond Drying

Buckling, black staining and mold under the boards become likely. Drying is usually no longer enough, and the bulk of the wet flooring has to be replaced.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Mold Under Boards

The space between hardwood and subfloor is dark and has no airflow. Growth there is invisible from above and shows up first as a musty smell.

Cracks From Rushed Drying

When the top of the wood dries much quicker than the inside, stress builds in the board. Boards may then split, crack or shrink apart for good.

Stains From Nails and Staples

Staples and flooring nails corrode when moisture lingers, and their grip weakens. Dark marks from that rust soak into the boards and can be too deep to sand out.

Sanding Too Early

Sanding cupped boards flat while they are still wet removes the raised edges. Once drying is complete, the middles sit higher and you have a crowned floor.

What We Do

Our Hardwood Floor Water Damage Process

1

Measure the Moisture

Our team meters the wet boards, the subfloor under them and a dry area of floor to compare against. That tells us how deep the water went and whether saving the floor is realistic.

2

Extract Standing Water

Rugs and furnishings come off the affected boards while pooled water is vacuumed up at once. Cutting off the water entering the joints early keeps swelling down.

3

Install the Mat System

We tape drying mats to the floor and attach them to a vacuum system, which draws moisture upward through the wood. Dehumidifiers control the room so the wood dries evenly without cracking.

4

Track Progress and Report

We track readings every day or two until boards and subfloor match dry reference areas. At that point you hear from us if it needs nothing, some replacement boards, or a refinish.
