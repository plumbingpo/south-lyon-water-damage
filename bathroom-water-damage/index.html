Bathroom Water Damage: What South Lyon Homeowners Should Know

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. Hidden from view, it builds up behind the wall tile and down in the subfloor. We help South Lyon homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

Warning Signs

  • A brown ring or sagging spot on the ceiling directly below the bathroom
  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity
  • A toilet that rocks slightly, or dark staining around its base
  • On the wall beside the shower, baseboard that has swelled or paint that is peeling
  • Inside the vanity, a base shelf that is stained, soft or out of shape
  • Edges of the vinyl floor peeling up, or dark discoloration showing through it
Why Speed Matters

The Damage Timeline

First days

Water Gets Past

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

Months one to three

Signs Start to Appear

You see darker grout, loosening tiles and blackened caulk. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months or more

Structure Weakens

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Pinpoint the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Cavities

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. Nothing is sealed back up until we have treated with antimicrobial and the readings show dry.

4

Rebuild and Seal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

The Room Below

Before dripping, water runs along joists and pipes, which can put the downstairs stain several feet away from the real leak. Its path can cross electrical wiring and ceiling lights.

Poor Ventilation

Steam lingers after each shower when the exhaust fan is weak or absent. It condenses on the ceiling and walls, encourages mold, and keeps already wet materials from drying.

Rot Under the Toilet

A failed wax ring leaks a little with every flush. It is category 3 water, and the wood surrounding the flange decays until the toilet wobbles.

Slow Vanity Drips

Behind the towels and bottles kept under the sink, a drain fitting or supply valve can drip for months. The cabinet floor swells, and the water runs through to the subfloor and the wall behind it.
