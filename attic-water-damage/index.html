Attic Water Damage Help in South Lyon

Water reaches an attic from two directions. Worn out shingles, flashing that has failed, and the seals around chimneys and vents all let rain in from overhead. Moisture also rises from the house below and condenses on cold roof sheathing when the attic is not ventilated well. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. In South Lyon, we inspect the attic, track down the source, and get it dry.

Most attic jobs center on wet insulation. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

What It Looks Like

  • After rain, a stain or brown ring on a top floor ceiling that looks worse
  • Daylight showing around a vent pipe, chimney, or along a roof valley
  • Streaks or dark staining on the roof deck's underside and on the rafters
  • Nail points coming through the sheathing that are rusty or beaded with water
  • Fuzzy white or black growth on the sheathing, usually thickest by the eaves
  • A musty smell in the attic or in the upstairs closets
The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that makes it through the roof falls into the insulation and is absorbed. With no sign inside the house, nobody notices the leak and the roof stays unrepaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold Can Start

Damp wood and paper facing in a warm, still attic are ideal for mold. The sheathing, along with the top face of the drywall ceiling, is where growth can begin.

A few weeks on

Stains Come Through

After repeated wetting, the insulation is saturated and water reaches the ceiling. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

Months later

Rot Sets In

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Mold spreads across large sections of the roof deck, and the rafters remain damp enough to decay.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

A Falling Ceiling

Insulation full of water weighs a lot. Drywall that has been wet from above weakens at the fasteners and can drop suddenly, bringing wet insulation down with it.

Lost Insulation Value

Once damp and packed down, insulation does little of its job and might not recover even when dry. The rooms beneath that section get colder or hotter, and you pay more for energy.

Wet Wiring

Cable runs, junction boxes and recessed light housings are all found in attics. When they get wet there is a risk of shock and fire, so keep out until the power has been checked.

Misrouted Exhaust Fans

Every day, a fan duct that stops short of the roof releases humid bathroom air into the attic. The condensation it leaves on the sheathing can pass for a roof leak.

What We Do

Our Attic Water Damage Process

1

Inspect and Find the Source

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show how far it reached.

2

Remove Wet Insulation

Saturated or moldy insulation is bagged and removed so the wood and drywall under it can dry. Rooms under the attic are protected, and the route in and out stays contained.

3

Dry the Framing

Rafters, sheathing and joists are dried by air movers and dehumidifiers until their moisture levels are normal. We clean visible mold off the wood, then apply an antimicrobial.

4

Correct the Source

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. Once the attic stays dry, new insulation goes in to restore the R-value.
